30 MW Thermal RDF Energy Recovery Facility

A constructed example demonstrating the type of analysis, deliverables and engineering depth produced in a waste-to-energy feasibility study.

This is a constructed illustrative example, not a client project. All parameters use published reference data and standard engineering correlations. It does not represent a recommendation, forecast or guarantee of project outcomes.

Project premise

A hypothetical project developer is evaluating a greenfield energy recovery facility in Southern Europe. The site has access to 120,000 tonnes per year of refuse-derived fuel (RDF) from a regional mechanical-biological treatment plant. The developer needs to understand whether the project is technically and commercially feasible before committing to FEED.

The study scope covers: feedstock characterisation, technology selection, mass and energy balance, emissions compliance assessment, equipment definition, and economic sensitivity analysis.

Technology selection

Three conversion routes were evaluated against the feedstock properties, scale and project requirements:

TechnologySuitabilityAssessment
Moving grate combustionSelectedProven at this scale and feedstock quality. Widest commercial reference base for RDF. Straightforward steam cycle integration.
Bubbling fluidised bedViable alternativeGood fuel flexibility but requires more stringent feedstock preparation. Higher parasitic load. Comparable CAPEX at this scale.
Gasification + gas engineNot recommendedLimited commercial track record for RDF above 10 MWth. Syngas cleaning requirements add cost and complexity. Better suited to clean biomass feedstocks.

Emissions and compliance

The flue gas treatment train is designed for compliance with the EU Industrial Emissions Directive (2010/75/EU) and associated BAT conclusions for waste incineration.

ComponentFunction

Estimated CO2 intensity: ~0.9 t CO2 / MWhe (gross), of which approximately 50–60% is biogenic (depends on RDF composition). The biogenic fraction may qualify for carbon credits under applicable national schemes.

Economic sensitivity

Capital and operating cost ranges based on published industry data and vendor reference information for grate-fired WtE plants at this scale.

Key sensitivities identified in the analysis:

  • Gate fee level is the dominant revenue driver. A 20 EUR/t change in gate fee shifts project IRR by approximately 3–5 percentage points.
  • Electricity offtake price determines viability in power-only mode. CHP with heat revenue significantly reduces the required gate fee.
  • Construction cost varies with site conditions, permitting timeline, and contractor market. Southern European greenfield sites are typically at the higher end of the range.
  • Waste supply security underpins the entire business case. A long-term waste supply contract (15+ years) is typically required for project financing.
These ranges are based on published reference data for comparable European WtE projects. Actual project economics depend on site-specific factors including land cost, grid connection, waste composition variability, permitting timeline, financing structure and applicable subsidies. A project-specific feasibility study quantifies these for the particular configuration and location.
